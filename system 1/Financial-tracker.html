      Guide book

      How to use Ledger, section by section
      1. Add income. Every time money comes in — allowance, pay, a transfer — enter the amount and record it. This is the number your budgets and balance are measured against.
      2. Add expense. Pick a category, jot down what it actually was (like "Nasi lemak" or "electricity bill"), the date, and the amount. The date defaults to today automatically. The description is optional but makes your history and CSV export far more useful than category alone — you'll be able to tell "Food" apart from "Food, but specifically the good roti canai place."
      3. Daily log. This is your day-by-day view for the current month — one row per day, showing that day's total spending with a bar so heavier spending days stand out at a glance. It updates the moment you record an expense, so you don't need to export anything to see how a day went; save the CSV export for a proper backup at month's end.
      4. Monthly budget — how it works. Choose a category and set how much you're allowed to spend on it this calendar month. Ledger automatically totals your expenses in that category for the current month and shows a bar filling up as you spend. The bar turns rust-red once you go over. Budgets reset naturally each month because they're always compared against that month's dated expenses — nothing to reset manually, just keep dating your expenses correctly.
      5. Reading the budget bars. Each row shows "spent / budget" for the current month. If you set a Food budget of $300 and you've logged $180 in Food expenses dated this month, the bar sits at 60%. Re-setting a category's budget overwrites the old amount for that month.
      6. Savings goals. Give a goal a name and a target amount. Progress is based on your overall balance (income minus all expenses), not a monthly figure — it's for longer-term targets like a laptop or a trip.
      7. Daily spending limit. A simple trip-wire: if a single day's expenses cross this number, you'll get an alert the moment you log the expense that pushes you over.
      8. Spending by category chart and History give you the shape of where money goes and a running log you can export to CSV any time.
      9. Reset for a new month. At month's end, tap "Export backup & reset." It automatically downloads a CSV of everything so far, then clears your income and expense totals back to zero — a clean slate for the new month. Your savings goals and their target amounts stay, though their progress bars will show 0% right after a reset since progress is based on your balance — they'll fill back up as you add income and keep saving. Budgets you set for past months stay saved under that month; only the running income/expense numbers are cleared.

      Everything is stored locally in this browser (localStorage). Clearing browser data or switching devices means starting fresh — export to CSV first if you want a backup.
