    How to use Ledger, section by section
    1. Add income. Every time money comes in — allowance, pay, a transfer — enter the amount and record it. This is the number your budgets and balance are measured against.
    2. Add expense. Pick a category, the date it happened, and the amount. Recording expenses on the day they happen (rather than batching them later) keeps your daily limit and monthly budget checks accurate.
    3. Monthly budget — how it works. Choose a category and set how much you're allowed to spend on it this calendar month. Ledger automatically totals your expenses in that category for the current month and shows a bar filling up as you spend. The bar turns rust-red once you go over. Budgets reset naturally each month because they're always compared against that month's dated expenses — nothing to reset manually, just keep dating your expenses correctly.
    4. Reading the budget bars. Each row shows "spent / budget" for the current month. If you set a Food budget of $300 and you've logged $180 in Food expenses dated this month, the bar sits at 60%. Re-setting a category's budget overwrites the old amount for that month.
    5. Savings goals. Give a goal a name and a target amount. Progress is based on your overall balance (income minus all expenses), not a monthly figure — it's for longer-term targets like a laptop or a trip.
    6. Daily spending limit. A simple trip-wire: if a single day's expenses cross this number, you'll get an alert the moment you log the expense that pushes you over.
    7. Spending by category chart and History give you the shape of where money goes and a running log you can export to CSV any time.
    8. Lock this ledger. Optional password gate for this device. It's a light deterrent stored in your browser, not encryption — don't rely on it for sensitive data.

    Everything is stored locally in this browser (localStorage). Clearing browser data or switching devices means starting fresh — export to CSV first if you want a backup.
